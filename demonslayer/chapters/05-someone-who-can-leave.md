# Someone Who Can Leave

Rengoku's first cut took the ceiling down.

He had aimed above the living strands, at the beam from which they hung. The blade passed through cedar with a dry roar. Tiles crashed into the empty room beyond, and for a heartbeat the weight of the falling roof dragged the threads away from Mio.

“Under the table!”

She dropped.

Rengoku turned his sword flat and met the descending lattice.

Metal screamed.

The strands bit into the back of his blade, drawing red sparks along its edge. His wounded ribs tightened as he forced air into his lungs.

Across the room, Akaza kicked a broken pillar through the web around the carpenter. The pillar broke into six neat pieces. He caught the man by the back of his coat and dragged him clear before the pieces struck the floor.

Then he drove his good fist into the boards beneath them.

The shock ran through the house.

Paper screens burst outwards. The table leaped. For an instant, every strand quivered loose.

Rengoku pushed through.

“Flame Breathing. Fourth Form—”

His sword swept around the table in a rolling blaze of motion.

“Blooming Flame Undulation!”

The threads striking from above met steel and recoiled. He could sever the free lashes. The taut ones running back towards the captives he turned aside.

It was slower. Harder.

A strand opened his cheek. Another sliced through the edge of his haori and pinned the white cloth to the wall.

He tore it free.

“Rui! Stop moving their bodies!”

“They will get hurt.”

“You are hurting them!”

Rui swept his hand across the room.

The carpenter stood abruptly, against his will.

Akaza saw the movement too late. A fist struck him in the mouth. He staggered. The carpenter's second blow came with a small, terrified apology.

Akaza caught the man's wrist.

His own teeth had cut the inside of his lip. Blood ran down his chin.

Rui lifted two fingers.

The man's elbow began to bend backwards.

Akaza let go at once.

The hand struck his face again.

Rengoku saw the expression that followed: Akaza's eyes narrowing, his weight settling onto the ball of his foot. The instant before a precise, devastating answer.

“He is not attacking you!”

Akaza's foot stopped.

He took the next blow on his forearm.

“I know.”

It came through clenched teeth.

Rengoku did not thank him. There was no time, and it was not a favour.

He drove his sword through a floorboard beside the table and levered upwards. The boards split, exposing the strands beneath them.

The house had a second shape under its beautiful surfaces.

Knots. Hundreds of them. Threads running from room to room, disappearing into walls, turning back on themselves. And everywhere, wooden name tablets caught like insects in a web.

“Mio. Look at me.”

She crawled out from beneath the table, shaking.

“Is this the way you came in?”

“I don't know.”

“Take your time.”

A thread struck his blade hard enough to numb his wrist.

“Some of your time,” he amended.

She looked past him.

“The lantern.”

Outside the first doorway, its amber light still fell across the veranda. The thread on its handle ran all the way back to Rui's hand.

The extra room had stretched around it.

The lantern had not moved.

Rengoku understood the distance then: the house kept putting itself between a person and the place they wanted to reach.

They could fight through a hundred rooms. It would make a hundred more.

“Akaza! We need to stop moving towards the door.”

“I had noticed the lack of progress.”

The carpenter's arm snapped out again. Akaza ducked, caught him around the waist and lowered him bodily onto his side. He trapped the man's sleeves beneath his own knees, spreading the cloth rather than twisting the joints.

The strain dragged a sound from his injured shoulder.

“What do you suggest?”

Rengoku looked at Rui.

“We speak to the person holding it.”

***

Rui watched from beneath a roof that no longer entirely existed.

Pieces of tile lay around his feet. One had cut his cheek. He touched the blood with a puzzled expression.

“You broke my house.”

Rengoku lowered his sword a fraction.

“Yes.”

“You came here to take them.”

“I came because I heard someone calling.”

Rui looked at the doorway. For an instant, his face was the face of a boy trying to remember whether he had left something outside.

Then his gaze hardened.

“They called you Father.”

Mio stood behind Rengoku.

“You made me.”

Rui's fingers closed.

The thread at her wrist tightened.

Rengoku stepped between them, but she caught his sleeve with her free hand.

“Please. I want to say it.”

He stopped.

She looked directly at Rui.

“I said the words you wanted because it hurt when I didn't.”

Rui stared at her.

“You smiled.”

“Yes.”

“You sat with me.”

“Yes.”

“Then why—”

His hand jerked.

Mio cried out. Rengoku moved, catching the strand against his sword guard before it could bite deeper. Blood welled at the edge of her sleeve.

Akaza looked up from the carpenter.

“You have your answer.”

“Be quiet.”

“You asked why.”

Rui's voice rose.

“You do not know anything about family.”

Akaza went still.

For a moment, Rengoku thought he had pushed too far.

Then Akaza released one of the carpenter's sleeves and held out his good hand, empty.

“There were people who cared for me when I had nothing to give them.”

“Then you should have stayed with them.”

“Yes,” Akaza said.

The word had no defence inside it.

“I should have.”

Rui looked away first.

At his feet, a piece of broken name tablet turned in the dust.

“I remembered mine,” he said suddenly.

Nobody moved.

“When I died. My mother. My father.”

The walls shivered.

For an instant, Rengoku could see another room through the torn paper. A smaller one. A bed. A cup beside it. The shape of someone kneeling, though there was no face he could make out.

Then Rui pulled his hand towards his chest, and the room disappeared.

“I remembered them,” he said again, fiercely. “That should have made this stop.”

Rengoku looked at the web beneath the floor.

“Remembering has not stopped your hands.”

Rui's fingers curled around the strand.

He saw them do it.

Very slowly, he looked at Mio's wrist.

“If you leave,” he whispered, “you will not come back.”

Mio's hand trembled against Rengoku's sleeve.

She did not answer.

Rengoku wanted to help her. To offer a promise that would quiet the room long enough for them all to get out.

He could hear how easily it would work.

You can visit him. Only once. Only until he feels better.

He looked at the blood on her sleeve and said nothing.

“I don't know,” Mio said at last. “I am not promising.”

The roof gave a long, tearing groan.

***

Rui screamed.

The web fell.

Rengoku shoved Mio behind the overturned table and turned into the first wave. His sword carved a burning circle through the free strands. The taut ones struck his guard like hammer blows.

Akaza pulled the carpenter underneath a fallen beam and crouched over him.

Threads cut across Akaza's back. His red vest darkened. He flung his good arm out, and a burst of force broke the descending ceiling into pieces small enough to fall around them.

One struck his shoulder.

He dropped to a knee.

Rengoku saw an opening through the web.

Rui's neck. Unprotected. A clear line, if he committed to it now.

He knew the distance. Knew the breath. Knew how quickly the blade could arrive.

“The wrist!” Akaza shouted.

Rengoku looked down.

The strand around Mio's wrist had become a red, rigid line. It ran across the room into Rui's closed fist. The smallest movement was pulling her towards the broken floor.

Rengoku abandoned the attack.

He caught her forearm with his free hand. Pain tore through his ribs as her weight struck him. His sword went down between two boards, braced against the thread, buying inches.

Only inches.

Rui crouched at the centre of the room, both hands clamped around what he held.

Rengoku could not reach him without letting go.

“Akaza!”

“I cannot move him yet.”

The carpenter's sleeve was pinned beneath the beam. Akaza was holding it off his chest with his injured shoulder. His good hand dug at the trapped cloth.

Rui could have stopped them both.

He had already done it.

“Look at her!” Rengoku said.

Rui shook his head.

“Look!”

Mio's feet slid over the splintered floor. Her breath came in short, shocked cries. The patch on her sleeve tore. Beneath it, Rengoku saw a second patch, older and carefully sewn.

A person who had repaired things until there was almost nothing left to repair.

“I don't want to stay,” she said.

It was barely a voice.

Rui lifted his head.

“I know,” he said, and sounded astonished.

His fingers opened.

For one heartbeat, the thread still held. It had been wound around his hand so many times that loosening his grip was not enough.

He saw that too.

Rui caught the single strand between his other thumb and forefinger.

He broke it.

Rengoku and Mio fell backwards together.

The room struck silence.

Beyond the overturned table stood the first doorway. No extra floor. No second table.

Their lantern burned on the veranda.

Rengoku helped Mio up. She looked at the clear space around her wrist as though it might close again if she moved too quickly.

“Can I go?” she asked.

Rui sat among his broken tablets.

His mouth worked.

“Yes.”

She went.

At the doorway, she stopped beside the lantern. Rengoku waited with her while Akaza freed the carpenter's coat and helped him sit upright.

“Daichi,” the man said, rubbing his throat.

Akaza looked at him.

“My name. Before this.”

“Can you stand, Daichi?”

He nodded, then looked at the door.

His legs would not move towards it.

Rui watched him. The old demand rose visibly in his face.

Nobody reached for a thread.

“I need a moment,” Daichi said.

Rengoku set the food parcel on the veranda and opened it.

“Then take one.”

He handed Mio a rice ball. She held it in both hands and took a bite so sudden and hungry that he looked away to give her room.

Daichi accepted another. He sat on the threshold, one foot outside, his back firmly against the doorpost.

“Don't call me Father,” he said.

Rui lowered his eyes.

“Daichi.”

The name sounded awkward. It was still the right one.

Rengoku turned towards Akaza.

Blood had reached the waistband of his trousers. His bandage had come loose.

“You need to sit down.”

“In a moment.”

“That seems to be going around.”

Akaza looked at Daichi, then at the open doorway, checking the distance.

Finally, he sat on the veranda.

Rui stayed inside.

No one invited him to supper. No one told him he had become good.

After a while, he began unwinding a thread from the leg of an empty chair.

It took him several attempts.
